Petit Protocol

Bowel perforation training for Malawian clinical officers · draft for Dr Jan Petit

PROTOTYPE · NOT FOR PATIENT CARE

Keep the teaching after the teacher flies home

Dr Petit trains clinical officers to find and repair bowel perforations using pig or cow intestine. This app is meant to hold his method: when to operate, how to sew, how to practise, and when to stop and refer.

It does not replace his hands in theatre. It repeats his workshop between visits and after he retires.

What a clinical officer can do here

  1. Walk a decision: repair, resect, stoma, or refer.
  2. Rehearse the repair steps in order.
  3. Set up a pig/cow intestine station the Petit way.
  4. Sit a short case test before a supervised list.
  5. Ask a question in simple English and get a conservative answer.
Training aid only. Do not use this app to operate on a patient. Follow your hospital protocol and a supervising surgeon when one is available.

Primary repair of ileal perforation

Standard LMIC teaching placeholder — replace with Dr Petit’s exact stitch and suture.

1
Resuscitate first. IV fluids, antibiotics that cover gut flora, nasogastric tube, catheter. Do not rush a shocked patient into theatre.
2
Midline laparotomy. Pack and suck. Look from duodenojejunal flexure to rectum. Count every hole. Look for paper-thin “pre-perforations”.
3
If one small antimesenteric ileal hole, bowel otherwise healthy, patient stable: freshen edges only if dead. Close transversely so you do not narrow the lumen.
4
Typical draft: interrupted seromuscular sutures (2-0 or 3-0 absorbable), one layer, bites in healthy bowel a few millimetres from the hole. Check lumen with a fingertip or a soft tube.
5
Wash the whole abdomen with warm saline until the effluent is clear. Place a drain only if your local protocol says so. Close fascia securely.
6
If many holes, dead bowel, hole next to the ileocaecal valve, or the patient is dying on the table: do not force a pretty anastomosis. Resect, or bring out a stoma, or pack and refer if you cannot finish safely.
The skill to practise on intestine is the stitch that does not leak and does not strangle the lumen. That is what the wet lab is for.

Pig or cow intestine station

This is the part Dr Petit already does in person. The app only standardises the station so a clinical officer can practise the week after he leaves.

What you need

  • Fresh small bowel from an abattoir or butcher (pig or cow). Rinse well.
  • Board, gloves, basic set: needle holder, forceps, scissors, 2-0/3-0 suture.
  • Bowl of water or dilute iodine. Bucket for waste.
  • Phone to photograph the finished repair for the trainer.

Exercises (20–30 minutes each)

  1. Single hole. Cut a 5–8 mm hole on the antimesenteric border. Close transversely. Fill with water. Watch for leak.
  2. Two holes close together. Decide: two repairs or a short resection?
  3. End-to-end join. Cut the bowel. Join without twisting the mesentery. Test with water.
  4. Narrowing check. If you cannot pass a fingertip, you sewed it too tight. Redo.
Animal tissue is not sterile theatre. Treat it as dirty skills practice. Wash hands. Dispose of tissue as meat waste, not in the clinical incinerator unless the hospital says so.
